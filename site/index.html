Free and open source (MIT). For macOS 26 Tahoe, on Apple silicon or Intel, with Paperclip running on this Mac or at an address it can reach.

What a tab can’t do

Paperclip runs as a local server, and you use it from a browser tab. Dispatch turns that tab into a Mac app that keeps watching after you close the window.

Notifications, window closed

When an agent asks a question, needs an approval, fails or hits a budget alert, you get a macOS notification. Click it and that decision opens. The app checks Paperclip every minute on its own.

If a task sits with an agent that isn’t working on it, the notification offers Wake up ‹agent› and then tells you whether Paperclip woke the agent or skipped it.

A number on the Dock

The Dock icon shows the same number as Decisions in Paperclip’s sidebar, so you can see what is waiting without opening the window.

Liquid Glass, if you want it

Paperclip fills the whole window, with no title bar. The app adds the Mac’s menus, shortcuts for back, forward, home and copy link, two-finger swipes, and an optional Liquid Glass sidebar.
