A Mac app that puts your Paperclip in its own window and tells you when an agent needs you, even with the window closed.

Free and open source (MIT). For macOS 26 Tahoe, on Apple silicon or Intel, with Paperclip running on this Mac or at an address it can reach.

What a tab can’t do

Paperclip runs as a local server, and you use it from a browser tab. Dispatch turns that tab into a Mac app that keeps watching after you close the window.

Notifications, window closed

When an agent asks a question, needs an approval, fails or hits a budget alert, you get a macOS notification. Click it and that decision opens. The app checks Paperclip every minute on its own.

If a task sits with an agent that isn’t working on it, the notification offers Wake up ‹agent› and then tells you whether Paperclip woke the agent or skipped it.

A number on the Dock

The Dock icon shows the same number as Decisions in Paperclip’s sidebar, so you can see what is waiting without opening the window.

Liquid Glass, if you want it

Paperclip fills the whole window, with no title bar. The app adds the Mac’s menus, shortcuts for back, forward, home and copy link, two-finger swipes, and an optional Liquid Glass sidebar.

Answer from your phone

Connect your own Telegram bot and each decision reaches your phone with its buttons: approve, reject, answer a question or reply with a comment. It works without Tailscale or a public address.

“Explain it to me”

One tap sums up a decision in a few lines: what the task is, what the agent did, what it asks and what each option means. It uses a coding CLI already on your Mac (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode or a command you choose).

The bot answers only the chat you link, and its token stays in your keychain.

Set up once, update in one place

It finds your Paperclip

On first launch the app looks for Paperclip on this Mac. If it is running, it opens it. If it is off, the app starts it, and if it is missing, it helps you install it. You can also type another address. Before asking for permission, it shows you what a notification looks like.

One button for both versions

A single button shows the version of the app and of Paperclip. The app downloads its own updates in the background and installs them only when you restart or quit it. Paperclip updates only when you ask.

If Paperclip stops answering, the window says so, keeps retrying and comes back by itself.

What you need

macOS 26 Tahoe or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. Paperclip running on this Mac or at an address this Mac can reach, for example over your local network or Tailscale. If you don’t have Paperclip yet, the app can start its official installer, which needs Node.js 24.11 or later.

Uninstall

Quit the app and move it to the Trash, or run brew uninstall --cask %%CASK_TOKEN%%. To also remove its settings, its saved session and its Telegram data, run brew uninstall --zap --cask %%CASK_TOKEN%%. The Telegram bot token lives in your login keychain: remove it in Settings → Telegram before uninstalling, or in Keychain Access.

Questions

Does it change my Paperclip?

No. It opens the Paperclip you already run and talks to it through the same API as Paperclip’s own pages. The server, its data and its agents keep running on their own, and closing the app stops nothing.

Do I need Tailscale?

No. If Paperclip runs on this Mac, the app finds it, and Telegram works without a public address. Tailscale is one way to reach a Paperclip that runs on another machine.

What about Android or iPhone?

The app is for the Mac only. Decisions still reach any phone through Telegram, with their buttons, while the app runs on your Mac.

Is it official?

No. Paperclip Dispatch is an independent project, not made, endorsed or supported by the Paperclip team.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. It is free and open source, under the MIT license.

What does it collect?

Nothing. It has no servers. It talks to your Paperclip, to GitHub to look for new versions, to Telegram only if you connect a bot, and to the CLI you pick for “Explain it to me” only when you tap it. That CLI sends the decision’s context to its own provider, with your account.
